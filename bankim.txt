# Bankim Chandra Chattopadhyay - Biographical Information
## Personal Overview
- **Full Name**: Bankim Chandra Chattopadhyay CIE (anglicised as Bankim Chandra Chatterjee)
- **Birth**: 26 June 1838
- **Death**: 8 April 1894 (aged 55)
- **Place of Birth**: Kanthalpara village, North 24 Parganas, Naihati
- **Place of Death**: Calcutta, Bengal, British India
- **Title**: Sahitya Samrat (Emperor of Literature) in Bengali

## Family Background
- **Father**: Yadav Chandra Chattopadhyay (Deputy Collector of Midnapur)
- **Mother**: Durgadebi
- **Grandfather**: Shiv Narayan Chatterjee
- **Ancestral Origin**: Deshmukho village in Hooghly District
- **Siblings**: Youngest of three brothers
- **Notable Brother**: Sanjib Chandra Chattopadhyay (novelist, author of "Palamau")
- **Family**: Orthodox Bengali Brahmin family

## Education
- **Early Education**: Hooghly Collegiate School and Midnapore Collegiate School (then Governmental Zilla School)
- **Higher Education**: Hooghly Mohsin College
- **College**: Presidency College, Kolkata
- **Bachelor's Degree**: Arts degree (1859)
- **University**: University of Calcutta (one of first two graduates)
- **Law Degree**: Obtained in 1869

## Career
- **Government Service**: Subordinate Executive Service
- **First Appointment**: Deputy Magistrate of Jessore (1858)
- **Later Position**: Deputy Magistrate & Deputy Collector (after 1863 service merger)
- **Retirement**: 1891 from government service
- **Career Path**: Followed father's footsteps in government service

## Literary Career
- **Early Publications**: Ishwar Chandra Gupta's weekly newspaper Sangbad Prabhakar
- **Literary Beginning**: Started as writer of verse before turning to fiction
- **First Novel Attempt**: Bengali novel submitted for prize (unpublished, did not win)
- **First Published Fiction**: English novel "Rajmohan's Wife"
- **First Bengali Novel**: "Durgeshnandini" (1865) - first ever novel in Bengali
- **Literary Magazine**: Founded Bangadarshan in 1872
- **Total Works**: Fourteen novels and numerous treatises in Bengali
- **Literary Genres**: Serious, serio-comic, satirical, scientific, and critical treatises

## Major Works

### Anandamath (1882)
- **Significance**: Landmark of modern Bengali and Indian literature
- **Genre**: Political novel
- **Plot**: Depicts Sannyasi (Hindu ascetic) army fighting British force
- **Theme**: Calls for rise of Indian nationalism
- **Source of**: Vande Mataram song
- **Publication**: First appeared in serial form in Bangadarshan magazine

### Vande Mataram
- **Language**: Highly Sanskritised Bengali
- **Concept**: Personifies India as Mother Goddess (Bharat Mata)
- **Music**: Set to music by Rabindranath Tagore
- **Historical Significance**: Prominent during Swadeshi movement
- **Current Status**: National Song of India
- **Cultural Context**: Draws from Shakti tradition of Bengali Hindus

### Other Notable Works
- **Rajsimha** (1881, rewritten 1893): Historical fiction
- **Shakuntala, Miranda ebong Desdemona** (1873): First comparative analysis of different literatures in Bengali

## Political and Social Views
- **Stance**: Noted for pro-British stance
- **British Rule**: Accepted legitimacy of British rule
- **Education**: Supported English education
- **Nationalism**: Works called for Indian nationalism, though ultimately accepted British Empire could not be defeated
- **Cultural Influence**: Personified India as Mother Goddess (Bharat Mata)

## Literary Significance
- **Recognition**: Widely regarded as key figure in literary renaissance of Bengal and Indian subcontinent
- **Innovation**: Broke away from traditional verse-oriented Indian writings
- **Influence**: Provided inspiration for authors across India
- **Academic Study**: Works studied in comparative literature programs (e.g., Jadavpur University)

## Notable Relationships
- **Ramakrishna**: Had a respectful relationship despite different educational backgrounds
- **Meeting**: Humorous exchange about Bankim's name (meaning "bent" in Bengali)
- **Philosophical Connection**: Ramakrishna evoked Krishna and Radhika as Purusha and Prakriti of Sankhya philosophy, knowing Bankim's fondness for Sankhya philosophy

## Intellectual Influences
- **Education**: Highly educated with knowledge of English
- **Philosophy**: Influenced by Oriental thoughts and ideas
- **Special Interest**: Fondness for Sankhya philosophy

## Historical Context
- **Colonial Era**: Lived during British colonial rule in India
- **Bengal Partition**: Vande Mataram became prominent during Swadeshi movement against Lord Curzon's partition of Bengal
- **Cultural Movement**: Part of broader Indian literary and cultural renaissance

## Legacy
- **Literary Title**: Known as Sahitya Samrat (Emperor of Literature)
- **Cultural Impact**: Vande Mataram remains India's National Song
- **Literary Revolution**: Pioneered modern Bengali novel and broke from traditional verse-oriented writing
- **Continuing Influence**: Works continue to be studied and inspire authors across India
